## Editorial direction

These are newly written drafts for Drift, not transcripts or adaptations of another creator's recording. NSDR and Yoga Nidra are related but not interchangeable: the first two sessions offer secular guided rest, while the third draws on Yoga Nidra elements such as intention, a rotation of attention and breath awareness.

Use a calm, conversational delivery, not a whisper or imitation of a known teacher. No claims that a session replaces sleep, treats insomnia, changes neurotransmitters or guarantees a health outcome. Have an experienced Yoga Nidra teacher review the bedtime script before release; these drafts are not a certified teaching protocol or medical advice.

## Recording and timing

- Narrate only the quoted passages in the scripts, never timestamps or production notes.
- Times are segment start points on the final master, not words to read aloud.
- Use approximately 85–105 words per minute, with natural pauses between sentences.
- Each passage starts at its timestamp; the remaining segment is intentional silence. Do not stretch speech to fill it.
- Two-minute segments intentionally contain longer silence. Record and listen to the full master to confirm that the pacing feels supportive rather than abandoned.
- Target runtimes include silence and ending fades. They are not measured audio durations yet.
- Start with voice only: no third-party music, bells, samples or nature recordings.
- Do not loop guided sessions, replay them automatically or introduce an end chime.
- Maintain intelligible, consistent volume; no sudden onset, clipped peaks, heavy noise gate or abrupt ending. Compare perceived loudness with Drift's existing sounds on headphones and a phone speaker.
- Retain a lossless master. Verify the final distributed file's duration, seek behavior, locked-screen playback, natural end and offline playback before app release.
- Final 10 seconds of each session are silent. Any underlying audio added later must fade out before that silence.

## User-facing safety copy

For rest in a safe, stationary place. Do not use while driving or operating machinery. Keep breathing comfortable; there is no need to hold or force your breath. You can keep your eyes open, change position, skip an instruction or stop whenever you wish. This is a general wellbeing practice, not medical care or a replacement for sleep.

## Rights and release checklist

Original content is the chosen sourcing approach, not a declaration that every future asset is cleared. Do not apply an MIT or Creative Commons label to recordings merely because Drift's code or another repository has that label.

For a human narrator, obtain a signed agreement identifying the script and recordings and covering:

- Commercial use in Drift on iOS and the web, including free and paid collections.
- Hosting, streaming, in-app offline downloads and distribution through app stores.
- Worldwide territory, agreed duration, fees and any continuing royalties.
- Editing, mixing, mastering, encoding, excerpting and accessibility transcripts.
- Ownership or an adequate written license for the performance and recording; credit and any permitted promotion.
- Permission for any subcontracted studio or additional performer contributions.
- No implied endorsement, voice cloning or AI voice training. Any such use requires separate, explicit permission.

For synthetic narration, use an authorized non-impersonating voice. Verify the actual provider/account terms for commercial output and voice use at generation time. Save dated evidence and any attribution requirements. Synthetic generation is not automatically MIT, CC0 or exclusive copyright ownership.

For every final asset, retain a rights record: asset title and identifier, script revision, narrator/voice provider, source, applicable agreement and version/date, allowed uses, attribution, restrictions, receipt if applicable and checksum of the approved master. Do not store credentials in the record.

Background audio, if added, needs its own rights check. "Royalty-free" alone does not establish permission to redistribute an audio collection.
