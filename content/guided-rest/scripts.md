# Drift — Guided-rest narration drafts

Read alongside production-brief.md. Times mark the start of each segment. Speak only quoted text, slowly and naturally, then leave silence until the next timestamp. Do not read headings, timestamps or these instructions.

## 1. A little space · NSDR

Target runtime: 10:00. Daytime rest with a gentle return. Each segment is one minute. Last segment ends at 10:00.

### 00:00–01:00 · Arrive

“Welcome to Drift. These next few minutes are for resting, without needing to fall asleep. Choose a safe place where you can lie down or sit with support. Not while driving. Let your eyes close if that feels comfortable, or leave them softly open. Nothing needs to be achieved.”

### 01:00–02:00 · Permission

“You can change position or stop at any time. If looking inward feels uncomfortable, listen to the sounds in the room instead. Feel where the surface beneath you meets your body. Let it take some of your weight. You do not need to make yourself completely still.”

### 02:00–03:00 · Natural breathing

“Notice that breathing is already happening. There is no count to follow and no need for a deeper breath. Perhaps you feel movement around your ribs, or your belly, or the air near your nose. Choose whichever is easiest to notice. Let the next breath find its own pace.”

### 03:00–04:00 · Face and shoulders

“Bring a little attention to your forehead. Then your eyes, your cheeks, and the space around your mouth. You might allow your jaw a little room. Notice your shoulders without asking them to change. If softening comes, let it come. If it does not, you can still rest.”

### 04:00–05:00 · Arms and hands

“Feel your right arm, from the shoulder down to the hand. Notice your palm and fingertips. Now your left arm, your palm and fingertips. Perhaps there is warmth, contact, or very little sensation. Any of these is fine. Let both hands be where they are.”

### 05:00–06:00 · Back and legs

“Notice your back against its support. Your hips. Your right thigh, lower leg and foot. Your left thigh, lower leg and foot. There is no correct sensation to find. Simply include these parts of you in your attention, then let the effort of noticing become a little lighter.”

### 06:00–07:00 · Whole body

“If it feels comfortable, sense your body as a whole. A body resting here, with breathing moving through it. You do not have to hold every part in awareness. Let the room, the surface beneath you, and your body all belong to this moment.”

### 07:00–08:00 · Quiet rest

“For a little while, there will be fewer words. Thoughts may arrive. You do not need to finish them or push them away. Whenever you notice that you have wandered, return to a point of contact beneath you, or to an ordinary breath. Begin again as gently as you can.”

### 08:00–09:00 · No score

“There is no score for this rest. Being distracted does not mean you have done it wrong. Notice one thing that is here now: a sound, the touch of clothing, or the movement of a breath. Let that be enough for the next few moments.”

### 09:00–10:00 · Return

“Begin to notice more of the room around you. Feel your fingers and toes, and move them if you wish. Let your eyes open when you are ready. Before sitting or standing, take your time and check how you feel. This session is ending. You can return to your day at your own pace.”

Production: allow the spoken ending to settle; last 10 seconds silent. Stop at 10:00.

## 2. Room to rest · NSDR

Target runtime: 20:00. Longer daytime rest with a gentle return. Each segment is two minutes. Last segment ends at 20:00.

### 00:00–02:00 · Settle safely

“Welcome to Drift. This is time for guided rest, not a task to complete. Settle somewhere safe and stationary, lying down or sitting with comfortable support. Do not listen while driving. You can keep your eyes open or let them close. Adjust anything that needs adjusting. There is no need to wait for the perfect position, and no need to stay in a position that hurts.”

### 02:00–04:00 · Stay in choice

“You remain in charge of this practice. You can move, skip an instruction, or stop whenever you need to. If attention inside the body does not feel helpful, notice a sound nearby, or look at something steady in the room. For now, feel one place where you are supported. Perhaps the back of your head, your hands, or your feet. Let that simple contact be your starting point.”

### 04:00–06:00 · Let breathing happen

“Notice a breath arriving, then leaving. You do not need to slow it down, hold it, or make it larger. If breathing feels comfortable to follow, stay with a few ordinary breaths. If not, return to the support beneath you. There is nothing special you need to feel. Each time your attention wanders, you can return to whichever point feels most neutral and easy.”

### 06:00–08:00 · Face, neck and shoulders

“Bring your attention toward your forehead and the space around your eyes. Notice your cheeks, your mouth, and your jaw. Let your tongue rest comfortably. Include your neck and both shoulders. Rather than trying to remove every bit of tension, simply notice what is present. A small amount of ease is welcome. No change at all is welcome too. You are not required to feel a particular way.”

### 08:00–10:00 · Arms and hands

“Notice your right shoulder, upper arm, elbow, forearm and hand. Allow time to feel the right palm and fingers, or simply think of them. Then move your attention to your left shoulder, upper arm, elbow, forearm and hand. Both hands can rest. Both arms can rest. If you would rather change position, do so slowly, then find a comfortable point of contact again.”

### 10:00–12:00 · Torso and back

“Let attention move to the places where your back meets its support. Notice your upper back, then the area around your waist. Include the front of your body: the small movements of your ribs and belly, without changing them. If these areas do not feel comfortable to notice, choose your hands instead. Let the next few moments be simple. Feel one point of support, and allow breathing to continue.”

### 12:00–14:00 · Legs and feet

“Notice your hips, then your right thigh, knee, lower leg and foot. Let attention reach the heel, sole and toes. Now your left thigh, knee, lower leg and foot. There is no need to compare the two sides. Sensation may be clear, faint, or absent. You can include each part without needing to find anything. Let your legs be supported, just as they are.”

### 14:00–16:00 · Open rest

“Now there is less to follow. You might sense the body as a whole, or stay with one comfortable point of contact. Sounds can come and go. Thoughts can come and go. You do not have to settle every question that appears. When you notice yourself getting caught in a thought, gently return to the room, the surface beneath you, or one natural breath.”

### 16:00–18:00 · Rest without checking

“For these next moments, see whether you can let go of checking how well you are resting. There is no particular experience you need to produce. You may feel quiet, restless, sleepy, or much the same as before. Whatever is here, you can meet it without turning it into another job. Let your attention be light. There is time for a little more silence.”

### 18:00–20:00 · Gentle return

“Begin to notice sounds in the room and the space around your body. Feel your hands and feet. If you wish, make a small movement with your fingers, then your toes. Allow your eyes to open in their own time. Before you sit or stand, pause and notice how you feel. You might stay where you are for another moment. This session is coming to an end. Return to the rest of your day slowly.”

Production: return cues begin at 18:00, leaving time for an unhurried transition; last 10 seconds silent. Stop at 20:00.

## 3. Nothing more tonight · Yoga Nidra

Target runtime: 20:00. Bedtime adaptation; no wake-up cue. Each segment is two minutes. Last segment ends at 20:00.

### 00:00–02:00 · Prepare for the night

“Welcome to Drift. This is a gentle Yoga Nidra practice for winding down at bedtime. Settle in a safe place where you can rest without needing to drive or do anything demanding. Arrange your pillow, blanket, or other support. Let your eyes close if that feels right. They can stay open too. You can move, change an instruction, or stop whenever you wish.”

### 02:00–04:00 · Support and intention

“Notice where your body meets the bed or the surface beneath you. There is no need to press down or become heavier. Just notice the support that is already here. If you would like, choose a simple intention for this time. Perhaps, ‘For now, I can rest.’ Or use words of your own. Say them quietly to yourself once, without needing to make them come true.”

### 04:00–06:00 · Right hand and arm

“Let attention move through the right hand. The thumb. First finger. Middle finger. Ring finger. Little finger. The palm of the hand. The back of the hand. The wrist. Lower arm. Elbow. Upper arm. Right shoulder. You are not being asked to move these places, only to notice or think of them. If you lose your place, return wherever the next words meet you.”

Production: leave short, distinct pauses between named areas; leave the rest of the segment quiet.

### 06:00–08:00 · Left hand and arm

“Now the left hand. The thumb. First finger. Middle finger. Ring finger. Little finger. The palm. The back of the hand. Left wrist. Lower arm. Elbow. Upper arm. Left shoulder. Both arms resting. Both hands resting. You do not need to hold all of this in your attention. One place at a time is enough.”

Production: match the unhurried spacing of the right-side sequence.

### 08:00–10:00 · Feet and legs

“Bring attention to the right foot. The toes. The sole. The heel. Right ankle. Lower leg. Knee. Thigh. Then the left foot. The toes. The sole. The heel. Left ankle. Lower leg. Knee. Thigh. Both legs, supported. If sensation is unclear, you can simply picture the area or silently hear its name. There is nothing to get right.”

### 10:00–12:00 · Back, torso and face

“Notice the back of your body: the places around your hips, your lower back, your upper back. Then the front of your body, only if that feels comfortable. Your belly. Your ribs. Your shoulders. Your neck. Your jaw. Your cheeks. Your eyes. Your forehead. Let attention become less precise now. You can return to your hands or the sounds around you whenever you prefer.”

### 12:00–14:00 · Ordinary breath

“Notice an ordinary breath. Perhaps a small movement, perhaps the feeling of air at your nose. Let breathing continue without counting or holding it. Each breath can be different. You do not need to make the next one longer. If following the breath is not restful, feel the pillow or blanket instead. Let one familiar sensation be enough.”

### 14:00–16:00 · A simple image

“If imagining feels comfortable, picture a quiet room with soft evening light. There is nothing waiting for you there, and nothing you need to arrange. Or let the image go, and feel the real support beneath you. You do not need to see anything clearly. Let words, images, and thoughts pass without needing to follow them to an ending.”

### 16:00–18:00 · Intention, then quiet

“You might return once to the intention you chose. ‘For now, I can rest.’ Or your own words. Then let even those words become quiet. You do not need to keep listening closely. If you remain awake, that is all right. If sleep arrives, you do not need to resist it. Let this time be rest, without asking it to become anything else.”

### 18:00–20:00 · Bedtime ending

“There is nothing more to follow tonight. Feel the support beneath you, if you wish, and let the guidance fall away. There will be quiet now. You do not need to reach for the phone when the recording ends. Rest in whatever way feels comfortable.”

Production: no reorientation, bell, announcement or autoplay after this passage. Leave the remaining segment quiet; last 10 seconds silent. Stop at 20:00.
